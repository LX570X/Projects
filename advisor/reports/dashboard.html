Equities: S&P 500 -1.11% to 7,718.94, its biggest fall since July 29; Nasdaq -1.88% to 26,692.87; info tech worst at -2.6%; Philadelphia Semiconductor -4.3%, a sixth straight loss. Verified against the BLS release itself, not just secondary coverage.
  • The SECOND leg was Asia, and it is not a CPI story Attribution matters here and I nearly got it wrong. The risk-off did NOT start with CPI: the Kospi had already fallen about 7% on Oct 13, with a sell-side sidecar, BEFORE the print, on AI and semiconductor worries. Then on Oct 15 Asia fell again - the Korea Exchange triggered a sell sidecar at 09:07:42 KST, the KOSPI 200 opened -5.59% at 5,673.01 from 6,008.81 and traded into the 5,500s, with a separate 20-minute circuit breaker and Samsung -9.05%. The Block explicitly tied BTC breaking below $81,000 to that Asian risk-off. There is also an AI-credit strand: CoreWeave -15.46% after a Gartner warning that 80% of neoclouds could disappear by 2030, and Oracle -8.83%. So this is a three-strand selloff - hot CPI, an Asian equity break, and an AI-debt derating - not one cause.

After the print it repriced to somewhere between about 35% and 50% for the Oct 27-28 FOMC - CME FedWatch around 50%, Yahoo 35% and 37%, Investinglive 47%, Mitrade 37% peaking near 52%, Polymarket spiking near 50% then settling around 30%. I am deliberately NOT quoting a point estimate: providers and timestamps disagree by 15 points and my own research threads returned four different ranges. Two things cut the other way: odds were PULLED BACK on the morning of Oct 15 after Fed officials sounded cautious, and the dollar gave back its 18-month high. A year-end hike is heavily priced (a 63-96% band across sources; Citi moved to forecasting a December hike).

The dollar index DID close at an 18-month high, +0.4% to about 102.54, its strongest since April 2025 - and then slipped to 102.06 early on Oct 15. Recording this because the headline version of Oct 14 is 'hot CPI, yields surged, dollar soared' and only one of those three held to the close.

No exchange outage, hack or halt occurred on Oct 14-15, so the forced selling was leverage, not infrastructure. One caveat carried from the research: Binance's own status page was not checked, and it is the venue alleged to carry most of that hourly burst.
