Context & catalysts

  • OIL ANSWERED: gasoline pressure is EASING, so September's energy spike is unlikely to repeat This was the gap I named yesterday and it is the variable that decides whether October's CPI repeats. Retail gasoline is FLAT: AAA has $4.3333 on Oct 14, $4.3344 on Oct 15 and about $4.33 on Oct 16 - a tenth of a cent over three days - and DOWN from $4.3612 a week earlier, though still +41% against $3.0674 a year ago. The supply response is visible in the EIA weekly released Oct 15: gasoline stocks BUILT 3.5m barrels to 208.3m against an expected 0.5m draw, with refinery utilisation up 0.8pp to 93.5% and crude runs up 135,000 b/d. High runs plus a gasoline build is what caps pump prices. So the +9.1% m/m gasoline line that drove over half of September's all-items rise is not repeating so far.
  • But DIESEL is where the tightness sits, and the headline crude draw was a hurricane The same EIA report runs the other way on distillate: stocks DREW 2.5m barrels to 102.6m against an expected 1.6m draw. And the IEA's Oil Market Report of Oct 14 cut 2026 world demand to -2.6 mb/d year on year from -2.5 mb/d - a DEEPER contraction - with diesel accounting for 25% of the decline, blaming restricted product availability and a deteriorating economy, while September global supply rose 2.5 mb/d to 102.3 mb/d. It calls the 2027 recovery 'fragile'. Crude headed for a weekly gain on Iranian tanker strikes in Hormuz plus a US crude draw of 8.56m barrels to 415.6m, the largest since February - but Reuters attributes that draw to Hurricane Isaias Gulf of Mexico shut-ins, which is a weather interruption more likely to reverse than a demand-driven tightening. WTI November settled $97.97 (+3.03%) on Oct 15; Brent December $105.92.
  • The energy risk has a DATED expiry in November, and it is not a de-escalation Iranian attacks on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz have surged to their highest level since the war began, with an Iranian official saying Tehran has changed tactics against vessels. Trump has posted that he will not attack Iran before the November 3 midterms - but that is a three-week pause, not a ceiling: a third carrier group and roughly 2,000 Marines are being sent with the buildup due to complete by November, and the WSJ reports Trump told aides he expects to resume bombing that month. Reopening diplomacy is STALLED on sequencing - Washington wants the nuclear file first, Tehran wants the strait and maritime security first. ING is quoted that the market is pricing 'a significant risk premium' and that removing it entirely would require some form of US-Iran agreement. Also worth flagging: yesterday's research surfaced a CNBC attribution of easing yields to 'Russian diesel supply hopes from a US-brokered energy ceasefire'. No such concluded ceasefire was found - the diplomacy is open - so that framing is not corroborated.
  • THE HAWKISH REPRICING HAS LARGELY UNWOUND - this reverses what I reported yesterday Yesterday I said October hike odds had repriced from 22% to somewhere in the 35-50% range. As of Oct 16, CME FedWatch reads 72.3% HOLD, which is about 27.7% for a hike - back near the 17-22% that prevailed before the print. The arc, reported as ranges because providers genuinely disagree: pre-CPI ~17-22%, a post-print spike to ~37-50%, Oct 15 ~25-35%, Oct 16 ~15-28%. December is the one consistent figure at ~90-92%, so the market has moved the expected hike out rather than cancelling it. The cause is named and well sourced: SF Fed's Daly told Reuters on Oct 14 that further hikes are 'more likely than not' but there is no need to act at Oct 27-28, adding that 'risks are not realities, and reacting on risk when you have time to make a good decision... feels imprudent', and she attributed the hot CPI mainly to gasoline. Vice Chair for Supervision Bowman said at the IIF in Bangkok the same day that the Fed has time and she sees no urgent need for further action. Cleveland's Hammack was the hawkish dissent on Oct 15. Chair Warsh, in Bangkok on Oct 16 with the IMF's Georgieva, declined to signal - 'I don't prejudge meetings' - and called October and December the 'crystallising moments'.
  • The FOMC blackout begins TOMORROW - no more Fed commentary before the decision The blackout for the Oct 27-28 meeting runs Saturday Oct 17 from 00:00 ET through Thursday Oct 29 at 23:59 ET, confirmed two ways - the Atlanta Fed's published blackout calendar and the FOMC's own external-communications policy, which sets the window from the second Saturday before a meeting. So Warsh's Friday appearance was the LAST on-record Fed commentary before the decision. Practical consequence: the dovish drift of the last two sessions is now frozen in place for eleven days, and the next thing to move October pricing will be data, not speakers.
  • That Fed channel is what drove the crypto bounce - confirmed, not inferred The 10-year fell to about 5.08%, its lowest since Sept 25 and down roughly 11bp, and the dollar index fell about 0.44% to around 102.00 after its Oct 14 18-month high. The Block and Blockhead independently make the identical causal chain - Bowman and Daly remarks, October odds falling on FedWatch, the 10-year to ~5.08%, BTC back to ~$82,060. Asian equities stabilised: the KOSPI closed +2.05% at 5,668.05 after Thursday's -7.57% rout to 5,554.22, when both the KOSPI and KOSDAQ hit circuit breakers on the same day for the first time since July 29; the KOSDAQ closed +4.46% and triggered a BUY-side sidecar, mirroring Thursday's sell sidecar.
